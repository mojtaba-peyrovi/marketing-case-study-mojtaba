How the table was built · evidence from the data
The row formula

ΔConversions = ΔSpend ÷ that channel's observed 2024 CAC. The CACs are the same full-year figures from the channel P&L above — every value is hand-verified by the unit-test suite (tests/test_metrics.py).

  • Meta · −€1.31M ÷ €68.97 = −19,000
  • Influencer IG · +€701k ÷ €37.35 = +18,750
  • Google Ads · +€525k ÷ €9.71 = +54,070
  • Influencer Podcast · +€247k ÷ €2.85 ≈ +87k naive, reported +25k–49k after a haircut for the 93× scale jump
  • TikTok · −€447k ÷ €69.99 = −6,400
  • Test budget · €298k at ~€40 blended = +7,500
What in the data justifies each move
  • Scale Influencer Podcast & Google Ads — they post the highest paid-channel ROAS in 2024 (15.17× and 3.80×) at the lowest CACs (€2.85 and €9.71). They are starved of budget at 0.05% and 1.3% of paid spend.
  • Cut Meta — €3.71M at 0.52× ROAS, with monthly CAC inflating +49% Jan→Dec (€60 → €90). The trend says the next euro is worse than the last; scaling makes the ratio worse, not better.
  • Kill TikTok — the only paid channel sitting in the wasted-spend quadrant of Q4 (above-median CAC and below-median trial-to-paid: €69.99 / 20.6%). Already paused after September; the data confirms the call.
  • Cap Influencer IG at +41% — within the +60% capacity guardrail, so the optimizer cannot dump all freed budget into a single channel.

The one assumption — constant marginal CAC — is flagged as a data gap (Q5). It is why the recommendation pairs this rebalance with an incrementality test rather than executing it standalone.

Column naming recommendations

The current schema works but would benefit from clearer naming before scaling to more analysts.
Current name Problem Suggested name
row_num Carries no analytical value. Row position is implicit in any table and changes if rows are reordered or filtered. Remove entirely, or replace with a stable record_id if deduplication is needed.
Conversion Channel 1 / 2 / 3 Numbers imply a ranking or priority. They actually represent hierarchy levels: broad type → category → specific channel. channel_type, channel_category, channel_name
Monthly Conversions
Yearly Conversions		 Sounds like "conversions aggregated by month/year." Actually means "subscribers who chose a monthly plan" vs "subscribers who chose a yearly plan." The time dimension is unrelated to the plan type. Also, "conversion" here means trial start — but elsewhere in the analysis "conversion" means a paying subscriber. Same word, two definitions. monthly_plan_starts, yearly_plan_starts
Monthly Paid Conversions
Yearly Paid Conversions		 Same ambiguity as above, plus "Paid" is vague — does it mean the plan is paid, or that the customer has paid? And the word "Conversions" overlaps with the trial-start columns above, even though only this pair matches the analysis-wide definition of "conversion" (a paying subscriber). monthly_plan_paid, yearly_plan_paid
Share Coupon Used

End-to-end process

How the analysis was structured, what was assumed, and what the limits are.
1 How the analysis was structured
  • Loaded and cleaned the Excel dataset (1,152 rows × 17 columns) in Python using pandas
  • Separated full dataset from the 434-row subset that has conversion and revenue data
  • Computed derived metrics: CAC, ROAS, trial-to-paid, revenue per conversion
  • Analysed along three dimensions: channel, market, and time (monthly)
  • Generated all outputs at Docker build time so no raw data is processed at runtime
2 Key assumptions made
  • CAC = spend ÷ (monthly paid + yearly paid conversions) — post-trial, not trial-starts
  • ROAS uses New Revenue (Invoice Date) — confirmed payments only, not trial starters who may never pay
  • UNKNOWN language-group and channel rows (~25%) are excluded from per-language-group analysis
  • Rows with null conversion data are excluded from trial-to-paid calculations
  • One anomalous coupon value (1.33 in PR / English / February) is clamped to 1.0
3 Methods and tools used
  • Python · pandas · numpy for all data manipulation (no Excel formulas)
  • Outlier detection on Marketing Spendings and Subscribers: sort the values, take the middle 50% (between the 25th and 75th percentile), and flag any month that sits more than 1.5× that range above or below it — a standard rule for spotting unusually high or low values without assuming a bell curve
  • matplotlib / seaborn for static charts; Chart.js for interactive dashboard charts
  • Flask web framework; Docker for containerisation; GCP Cloud Run for hosting
  • Automated data validation and 11 metric unit tests run at every build
4 Limitations and uncertainties
  • Single year of data — cannot distinguish seasonality from trend
  • No customer-level data — all analysis is at channel × month aggregation
  • 62% of rows lack conversion data — channel efficiency analysis covers only the tracked subset
  • Budget reallocation projections assume each channel's cost-per-subscriber stays flat as spend grows — in reality the next euro spent is usually less efficient than the last (cheapest audience exhausts first, ad auctions get more competitive), so projected gains are best-case, not guaranteed
  • No LTV or churn data — recommendations are based on acquisition cost only

Metric definitions

Every number in this report uses one of these formulas — no exceptions.
What we mean by "Conversion"
Conversion = a new paying subscriber (Monthly Paid + Yearly Paid)
Throughout this report, "conversion" = paid subscriber. The dataset's Monthly Conversions and Yearly Conversions columns actually count trial starts, and are only used in Trial-to-Paid Rate. Everywhere else (CAC, "€X CAC"), the denominator is Monthly Paid + Yearly Paid Conversions.
CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)
Marketing Spend ÷ (Monthly Paid Conversions + Yearly Paid Conversions)
Why paid conversions, not all subscribers? A subscriber who starts a free trial but never pays costs money to acquire but generates no revenue. Using only confirmed paying subscribers gives a true picture of acquisition efficiency. This is the industry-standard definition of Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) for subscription businesses.
~€21
if we divide by all trial starters
vs
€68.97
if we divide by paying customers only ← we use this
Meta 2024: €3,714,189 spend ÷ 53,855 paid conversions = €68.97 per conversion
Return on Ad Spend (ROAS)
New Revenue (Invoice Date) ÷ Marketing Spend
Why Invoice Date? Invoice Date counts only revenue from customers who actually paid — confirmed cash received. Create Date would include trial starters who never convert, overstating how much a channel earns. A ROAS above 1.0× means the channel returns more in confirmed revenue than it costs.
Example: German-language audience invoice revenue ÷ €3,309,947 German-language spend = {{ "%.2f"|format(d.markets[0].roas) }}× ROAS
Blended ROAS
Total New Revenue (Invoice Date) ÷ Total Marketing Spend
Same formula as channel ROAS, applied company-wide. Sums all language groups and channels. Useful as a single top-line efficiency number for the CEO — but hides the variation between channels, which is where the actionable insight lives.
Example: €{{ "{:,.0f}".format(d.kpis.total_rev_invoice) }} total invoice revenue ÷ €{{ "{:,.0f}".format(d.kpis.total_spend) }} total spend = {{ d.kpis.blended_roas }}× blended ROAS
Trial-to-Paid Rate
Yearly Paid Conversions ÷ Yearly Conversions × 100
What does this measure? Of all users who started a yearly trial through a given channel, what percentage went on to pay after the 30-day trial ended. A high rate means the channel attracts users who genuinely want the product. A low rate means the channel attracts curious but uncommitted users — the trial converts cheaply but the payment does not follow.
Example: 37,711 Influencer IG yearly paid ÷ 129,965 Influencer IG yearly trials = 29.0% trial-to-paid
Spend Growth % (efficiency gap)
(December value ÷ January value − 1) × 100, calculated separately for spend and for paid conversions
What does the gap show? If spend and conversions grew at the same rate, every additional euro invested would return the same efficiency as the first euro. When spend grows faster than conversions — {{ ef.spend_growth_pct }}% vs {{ ef.conv_growth_pct }}% — each new euro is buying fewer conversions than the previous one. That is the definition of declining acquisition efficiency.
Spend: Jan €347,812 → Dec €593,799 = +{{ ef.spend_growth_pct }}%  |  Paid conv.: Jan 9,750 → Dec 12,909 = +{{ ef.conv_growth_pct }}%

Three concrete signals that this is a tracking gap, not a business reality
  1. English market is the obvious outlier. Four of its five active paid channels (Google, TikTok, IG-Influencer, Influencer Podcast) report zero spend across all 12 months while still booking revenue. This is implausible operationally — it points to ad accounts that were never connected to the spend ETL.
  2. Youtube Ads has zero spend in all three markets but ~€11k of revenue. Uniform across markets is a tell-tale sign of a category-naming mismatch — Youtube spend is almost certainly being booked under "Google Ads" at source (Google Ads UI bundles them) and never re-classified in this dataset.
  3. Influencer Podcast is mostly 0-spend across all markets. Consistent with industry norms — many podcast deals are barter, gifted, or revshare and may genuinely have €0 cash spend. This subset is real, not a tracking bug.
Why English ROAS specifically is unreliable
English reports ROAS = 0.87, but 64% of its revenue (€210k of €330k) has no matching spend in the dataset. The reported figure is computed almost entirely from Facebook/Instagram Ads (the only channel with any tracked spend) — meaning English's ROAS effectively measures one channel's performance, not the whole market. Any like-for-like comparison with German (ROAS 1.17) or French (0.85) is invalid: we are comparing a near-complete picture in DACH to a quarter-picture in English. Recommendations to scale or cut English-market spend should be paused until the data is reconciled.
Recommended actions
  1. Audit the spend ETL: confirm every English-market ad account (Google Ads, TikTok Ads Manager, Meta IG-Influencer line items) is connected and that spend rows are joined on the same channel-3 keys used by conversion data.
  2. Fix the Youtube Ads/Google Ads mapping at the source — either split YT spend out of Google Ads invoices or remap "Youtube Ads" conversions under "Google Ads" consistently across the warehouse.
  3. Tag known non-cash channels (barter podcasts, gifted influencer episodes) explicitly so future analysis can distinguish "no spend" from "spend missing".
  4. Until fixes ship: report English ROAS only at the Facebook/IG channel level, and flag the market in dashboards as "partial coverage". Use German + French for any cross-market comparison.
  5. Add a CI check to the data pipeline: any row with Conversion Channel 1 = Paid AND Marketing Spendings = 0 AND Revenue > 0 should raise a warning to the data team (added already).
